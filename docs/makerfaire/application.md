# Maker Faire Bay Area 2026 — Application

## Name

Hideyuki Takei

## Project Name

DINO EGG Catch Challenge

## Project Description

Meet a friendly long-necked dinosaur robot and try to catch a dinosaur egg with its mouth! Created by Kanata the Kid Creator and his dad, this hands-on project combines a LeKiwi mobile base, an SO-ARM101 robotic arm, and custom dinosaur decorations. We are developing driving, leader-arm teleoperation, and autonomous demonstration modes so children and families can explore different ways to control a robot. Planned prizes include NFC-enabled dinosaur keychains and KachiButton, a three-key USB keyboard keychain designed to work with a real PC. Our goal is to turn robotics and making into a playful experience visitors can understand, try, and take home.

## Please describe your hands-on activity.

We are developing three ways to experience the DINO EGG Catch Challenge. In Drive Mode, participants steer the dinosaur-shaped LeKiwi robot toward an egg capsule with a directional controller, then press a catch button to start an assisted pickup with the SO-ARM101 neck and mouth. In Puppet Mode, participants move a leader arm by hand to control the follower arm forming the dinosaur's neck and head. Scheduled Autonomous Mode demonstrations are planned to show the robot finding, approaching, and collecting an egg.

The activity is designed for children and families, with staff supervision and a clearly separated robot play area. Our prize plan includes 100 KachiButton three-key USB keyboard keychains placed inside egg capsules, plus approximately 100 NFC-enabled dinosaur keychains.

## What are you powering?

The LeKiwi robot and SO-ARM101 arm will run from AC adapters rather than batteries. Our current equipment plan includes one laptop or compact PC, two displays, the robot and arm, and small low-voltage accessories such as cameras and LED indicators.

Our preliminary planning estimate is 300–500 watts total on 120V AC. We do not plan to use heaters or other heavy electrical equipment.

## Describe additional details of your RF use.

The current plan is to use short-range consumer Wi-Fi for local robot communication and Bluetooth if required by the attendee controller.

Local control is intended to work without internet access. Any optional cloud-based AI demonstration would require a separate internet connection and is not part of the offline operating claim. We will coordinate wireless use with the event organizers and keep emergency stopping independent of cloud connectivity.

## Describe any safety and/or fire issues.

This interactive exhibit uses a small mobile robot and an articulated arm. Our safety plan calls for continuous staff supervision, low-speed operation, a clearly marked play area with a low physical boundary, and an accessible emergency-stop control. Participants will operate the controls from outside the robot's operating space. Arm speed, force or torque limits, and range of motion will be set and verified before public operation.

The robot and arm will use AC adapters rather than batteries. Power cables will be routed and secured to avoid trip hazards, wheel entanglement, and connector strain, and the robot's movement will be limited to the managed cable area. Decorative parts and prize-filled plastic egg capsules will be tested for secure attachment, handling, and safe operation. Staff will stop the robot if anyone enters the operating area. We do not plan to use flames, chemicals, compressed gas, sharp tools, or hazardous materials.

## Is there anything else you want to tell us?

We have completed the robot assembly and are customizing it into a friendly dinosaur, including the body, neck covers, head, and egg-catching mouth. The complete exhibit and its operating modes are still being developed and tested.

This is a project by Kanata the Kid Creator and his dad. We want to share how an existing robot can become an approachable, playful creation through mechanical design, 3D printing, controls, and experimentation. We are also preparing two kinds of take-home prizes: KachiButton, a three-key USB keyboard keychain, and original NFC-enabled dinosaur keychains. We are excited to bring the project to Maker Faire Bay Area and share what we have learned with children, families, and fellow makers.
